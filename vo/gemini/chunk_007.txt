NARRATOR: Her window smells of hot pastry and onions. A queue of hungry men forms outside it every noon, and she says they taught her everything she knows about haggling. Peg tells it like the best night out of her life. Her first husband was a nail-maker with a face like a boiled ham and about as much conversation. Peg loved a cooper named Alf, and had done for two years. Nobody in her position could afford Parliament, so they did what people in her position did. They held a Smithfield bargain. The name comes from London's great meat market, and it fits. The bellman went round the streets that morning, ringing and shouting that a wife would be sold at noon. Her husband tied a rope of straw round her neck and led her to the Bull Ring. He offered her to the crowd like a heifer. Alf, who had known the plan for a week, bid four shillings. Nobody dared bid higher. The husband took the money, signed a scrap of paper, and the three of them walked to the pub and drank to it.
PEG: "Nobody cried,"
NARRATOR: Peg says.
