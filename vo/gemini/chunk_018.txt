NARRATOR: Thursday comes. The Bull Ring at noon is ordinary and loud. Cattle low in their pens. Stallholders shout about turnips. Somebody, somewhere, is selling pins. The whole square smells of dung, wet straw and frying onions. And in the middle, where a crowd has formed the way crowds do when someone is about to be humiliated, there is a clear space, and it's meant for you. Ned ties the rope of straw round your neck himself. He does it the way he ties up a horse at the Hen and Chickens. Quick. Gentle. Without looking. He's very good with animals. He leads you into the middle by the loose end. People step back to let you through. A child laughs. Another child asks what you did. The straw scratches your neck. It's a small, stupid discomfort, and for some reason it's the only thing in the world you can concentrate on. Your daughter is in the crowd, twelve years old, holding your son's hand. He's nine. Peg is there too, shoving through the elbows with her hand over her mouth. When she reaches the front, she shouts something at Ned that you can't print. He pretends not to hear her. It's the only thing he has ever pretended well. Ned clears his throat. He's been practising.
