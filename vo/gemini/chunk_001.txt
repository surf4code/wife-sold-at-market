You're born in Birmingham in 1801, in a back room that smells of brass filings and boiled cabbage. Your father takes one look at you and asks the midwife whether the next one will be a boy. You are an hour old, and you have already disappointed the family business. The business is buckles. Your father is a journeyman in a workshop where forty men hammer tiny bits of metal for customers who will never know their names. Your mother polishes the finished ones at the kitchen table, by candlelight, for a few pence a dozen. Between them they earn enough to eat. Not one penny more.
